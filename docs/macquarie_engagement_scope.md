# Macquarie Group – Financial Statement Extraction Engagement

Macquarie's investment and advisory teams analyse hundreds of public company financial reports each quarter — US SEC filings, Chinese A-share disclosures, Japanese TSE earnings releases, and European annual reports — as part of deal evaluation, portfolio monitoring, and research. Today, extracting balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow data from these PDFs into usable spreadsheets is entirely manual: analysts open each report, locate the relevant tables, and copy data row by row into Excel, translating non-English labels and aligning columns by hand. A standard English 10-K takes 30 to 45 minutes; a 350-page Japanese filing under J-GAAP can take three to four hours and requires a language specialist. This does not scale, introduces transcription errors that propagate silently into downstream models, and wastes analyst time on mechanical work rather than the interpretation and judgement they are hired for. Non-English filings are frequently deprioritised — not because the data is unavailable, but because extracting it is too expensive in analyst hours — creating an information gap in the firm's global coverage.

To address this, Macquarie required a Copilot Studio agent that accepts any PDF financial report — regardless of language, accounting standard, or document length — and automatically extracts all three core financial statements, translates every row label into English while preserving the original language for traceability, maps the data to a common IFRS/GAAP taxonomy, and delivers the output as a professionally formatted Excel workbook with separate sheets for each statement and correctly aligned multi-period columns. The agent is embedded in Microsoft Teams so that analysts can upload a PDF in chat and receive a downloadable workbook within minutes, without leaving their existing workflow. The solution reduces extraction time from hours to under two minutes per report, achieves 98.6% accuracy across benchmark reports spanning four languages and four accounting standards, and removes the bottleneck that prevented the team from processing non-English filings at scale — enabling truly global coverage in quantitative analysis without proportional headcount investment.
